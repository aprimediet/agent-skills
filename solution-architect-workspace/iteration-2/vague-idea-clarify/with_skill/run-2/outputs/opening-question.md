# Opening Clarifying Question

> Solution Architect | Step 1 anchor question

"A notification system" can mean very different things depending on scope. To anchor the conversation, the single most important question to ask first is:

**What channels does this notification system need to deliver through — in-app (notification center / bell icon), email, SMS, push (mobile/web), or some combination — and which is the primary one?**

## Why this question first

Channel choice is the highest-leverage scoping decision. It determines:
- Which delivery capabilities exist (each channel has different inputs, failure modes, and edge cases).
- Whether user preference management is needed (multi-channel implies per-channel opt-in/opt-out).
- Whether delivery tracking and retries matter (email/SMS/push can bounce or fail; in-app rarely does).
- The shape of templating (rich HTML email vs. short SMS vs. structured in-app payload).

Everything downstream — preferences, templating, batching, delivery status — hangs off the answer to channels.

## Assumed answer for this non-interactive run

Since this run is non-interactive, the question is answered with a clearly-stated assumption:

> **Assumption:** A multi-channel notification system for a typical web SaaS, with two channels at launch — **in-app notifications** (a notification center / bell icon inside the product) and **transactional + digest email**. SMS and mobile push are treated as out of scope for v1 but the design leaves room to add channels later. The audience is end users of the SaaS product, with notifications generated by application events (e.g., account activity, mentions, status changes, billing events).

These assumptions are carried into the capability breakdown and recorded under Open Questions in the final document.
